## Context

CLSA already models several Chilean statutes: Ley 20.215 Monday/Friday *observance* shifts (San Pedro y San Pablo, Encuentro de Dos Mundos, Iglesias Evangélicas), Ley 20.299, Ley 20.983 (Sep 17 / Jan 2 bridges via `fixedOnWeekday`), and Ley 21.357 (solstice Indigenous Peoples' Day, as a table). The remaining September "sandwich" bridges — Sep 17 when Sep 18 = Tuesday, and Sep 20 when Sep 19 = Thursday — were modeled as historical one-offs and dropped for future years, under the (incorrect) assumption they were single-year laws.

They are in fact **Article 35 ter of Ley 20.215**, a permanent provision. The existing `fixedOnWeekday` rule kind (added for Ley 20.983) already expresses them, because the weekday conditions are equivalent to fixed offsets:
- Sep 18 = Tuesday ∧ Sep 19 = Wednesday ⟺ Sep 17 = Monday
- Sep 18 = Wednesday ∧ Sep 19 = Thursday ⟺ Sep 20 = Friday

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- Model Art 35 ter as permanent rules on CLSA (inherited by CLBA).
- Remove the now-redundant historical one-off bridge entries.
- Restore full CLSA parity with python-holidays 0.99 (the prior change's intentional ≥2026 divergence is eliminated).

**Non-Goals:**
- No new rule kind (`fixedOnWeekday` already exists).
- No change to Ley 20.983 (Sep-17-Friday / Jan-2) or any other calendar.
- No change to the solstice Indigenous Peoples' Day table.

## Decisions

### D1. Reuse `fixedOnWeekday`, gated `fromYear = 2007`
Add `fixedOnWeekday(9, 17, Monday, 2007)` and `fixedOnWeekday(9, 20, Friday, 2007)` to `santiagoRules()`.

*Why 2007:* Ley 20.215 was published in September 2007. Validated empirically against python-holidays 0.99: with this gate the rules emit exactly python-holidays' set — Sep-17-Monday `{2007, 2012, 2018, 2029, 2035, 2040, 2046}` and Sep-20-Friday `{2013, 2019, 2024, 2030, 2041, 2047}` — and correctly exclude the pre-law weekday matches (2001 Sep-17-Mon, 2002 Sep-20-Fri).

### D2. Remove the redundant historical one-offs
Delete from `chileOneOffHolidays()`: 2012-09-17, 2013-09-20, 2018-09-17, 2019-09-20, 2024-09-20 — all now generated by the rules. Keep 2010-09-17 / 2010-09-20 (bicentenario; Sep 18 2010 = Saturday, so neither Art 35 ter case applies and Ley 20.983's `fromYear=2017` excludes them), 2017-04-19 (census), 2022-09-16 (plebiscite), and the solstice table.

### D2b. Gate Día de las Iglesias Evangélicas to fromYear = 2008 (related fix)
Extending CLSA to `fromYear = 2007` (D1) made years 2007–2009 meaningful and exposed a pre-existing gap: the Oct 31 *Día de las Iglesias Evangélicas* rule had no validity window, so it (and its Friday observance shift) generated a spurious 2007-11-02. Ley 20.299 created that holiday in 2008, so the rule is gated `fromYear = 2008`. Small, clearly-correct fix included here because it is exposed by, and required for, this change's full-parity goal.

### D3. Supersede the prior "drop future bridges" decision
This change reverses decision D3 of `clsa-ley-20983-bridge-rule`. Those bridges are permanent law, so projecting them is correct, not speculative. The spec's "No speculative future bridge projected" constraint is removed.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **Behavior change** → CLSA/CLBA gain Sep-17-Monday / Sep-20-Friday holidays for qualifying years ≥ 2026 (and 2007). Mitigation: cross-check vs python-holidays 0.99 must show **zero divergence in both directions** over 2010–2050; add explicit tests (2029 Sep 17 present, 2030 Sep 20 present, a non-qualifying year absent, CLBA inheritance).
- **fromYear correctness** → If 2007 is slightly off, early years would diverge. Mitigation: the empirical match against python-holidays (including the 2001/2002 exclusions) confirms the gate.

## Open Questions

None — the rule text, the `fixedOnWeekday` mapping, and `fromYear = 2007` are all confirmed (the latter validated against python-holidays).
